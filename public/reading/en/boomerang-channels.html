At Boomerang

“I’ve paid” and “We don’t have the order” can both sound true. The distance between them is where support time disappears.

Who has the order?

Managing Boomerang’s online sales channels meant thinking beyond a single checkout screen. Follow one order from payment to confirmation and ask what information is in each team’s hands.

In this example the bank shows a successful payment, but the store has no receipt. Asking for a second payment risks charging twice. Sending an order straight to picking leaves the team unsure what it just confirmed.

Leave a handoff the next person can read

Keep the payment reference, amount and time with the order. Record who will reconcile it and when the customer should hear back. “Followed up” gives the next person very little to go on.

The account was debited but no receipt arrived. First move?

Create a second order immediately It may hide the first problem and leave two orders to reconcile later.

Match the payment reference to the order It identifies the transaction in question and avoids asking for another payment while it’s being checked.

A usable handoff lets the next colleague read the last entry and tell whose turn it is and what the customer is waiting for.

The money left the account. Who follows up?

You’ve checked out and the only thing on your phone is a debit alert. You give support the amount, the time and the bank reference if you have one. Pick what the team knows and follow that same payment.
