What exactly will you do for the shop?

A shop owner asks whether you can get some products onto the website. AI can help draft the descriptions. Someone still has to match each photo to its product, check the specifications and hand over a usable file. In a one-person business, that’s you. A clearly scoped offer tells the buyer what they’ll get and tells you what you’ve agreed to do.

Open an offer whose quality you know how to judge. Who needs that work, and what’s the smallest sample you could show them?

What could you deliver?

Choose a starting point or search. Each card explains a proposed buyer, quality evidence, AI’s role and a first experiment. These are editorial offer ideas, not established demand or promised earnings.

If several offers appeal to you, which one would let you catch a bad AI draft? Someone who knows a shop’s range may immediately spot a description written for the wrong model. For the kinds of work I’ve done myself, see my CV.

A one-off order or a job that comes back every month?

Back at the shop, ask what took longest the last time a product went online. The descriptions might be ready. Perhaps the supplier’s file won’t import. The SBA guide to market research suggests talking to buyers directly and finding out what alternatives they use today.

Now you can offer a file the shop can import for one product category, with every photo and specification checked against its list. If it adds stock every week, an ongoing arrangement may fit. Read the tabs with that difference in mind.

Bounded project

When the finish line is clear

For one category, agree which file you’ll receive, what you’ll return and the number of revisions. After the first sample, compare your estimate with the time it actually took.

Recurring work

When the need genuinely repeats

If a new batch arrives every month, a retainer is worth considering. Agree how many products you’ll handle, how soon you’ll reply and what counts as extra. The arrangement still depends on the customer needing the work next month.

Initial experiment

When the problem is still unclear

If you’re unsure where the shop gets stuck, get permission to try one row. Did it import? Did the photo and specifications land in the right places? Decide whether to change the offer, continue or drop it based on what you find.

How can a sample lead to a first order?

How can you tell whether a buyer wants this? A compliment about a sample is encouraging. A shop sending its own file for a trial tells you something more specific. Open each stage to see what you need before taking the next step.

Clarify the problem with a real potential buyer

Ask the person who actually lists products to walk you through their last upload. Where it gets stuck and who signs off the file may surprise you.

Make a permitted, reviewable sample

Build a sample with a file you have permission to use, or clearly labelled fictional products. Record how long it takes and what still fails. Get permission and remove confidential details before sharing client work.

Complete one clearly scoped handover

For that first category, agree on the input, revision rounds and how the shop will accept the file. Time the calls and file exchanges too. You’ll have a better estimate next time.

Standardize what actually repeats

If the file uses the same columns each time, save a template. Flag unusual products for review. Before automating a step, try restoring the correct file after a mistake. See my solutions for related work.

What if the photo or specification is wrong?

For the shop, evidence of a good delivery is tangible. Import a row and check that the right product appears with the right photo and specifications. If a tool has guessed a specification, who catches it? OpenAI’s guide discusses limiting access and handing sensitive actions or errors to a person.

Take one product all the way through. Do you have permission to use its data? Can the buyer check the result? If it’s wrong, who fixes it? Those answers will help you decide whether to take the next order.

AI makes preparation faster, but you still lack enough time to deliver the orders. What is a useful next step?
