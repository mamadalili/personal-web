The AI Model Market

When you pay for an AI tool, who gets the money? Follow a payment through ads, subscriptions and model training, then see how investors put a price on the companies involved.

What do the models do?

A model may have chosen the ad you saw on a website today, even if you never opened a chat. In the reference estimate, ad ranking is a bigger business than language models. Pick an application in the chart to see what people are paying for.

Who plays in each field?

Look at that ad from the supplier’s side. Google works on ad ranking, language models, cars and drug discovery. A specialist might spend all its time on just one of those jobs. That’s why a company can turn up under several headings here.

How much do the labs sell?

Annualised revenue asks what a year of sales would look like at the pace recorded on a particular date. Actual sales over twelve months can differ. Check the date beside OpenAI or Anthropic, then look at valuation for a different question: what price did investors put on the company?

Coding: the model or the tool?

Say you subscribe to Cursor to write code. You pay Cursor, and Cursor may spend part of your payment on a model. The same money can show up in two companies’ sales. Adding tool and model revenue can therefore count it twice. Copilot and Replit also sit in the tool layer.

What does training a model cost?

Before anyone asks a model a question, its builder pays to train it. Epoch AI’s selected estimates run from a few thousand dollars for GPT-2 to about half a billion for Grok-3. Those figures cover a training run. Paying the team or serving your next request is a separate expense.

Thousands and half a billion, on one scale

Customer money or investor money?

Your subscription counts as a sale. An investor puts in money for a stake in the company. Pick a company to see how much it raised in a particular round and what sales it reported around that time. To work out its profit you’d also need its costs.

How many times sales is it worth?

For a small example, a business valued at $20 with $2 in annualised sales is priced at ten times sales. The chart makes that same comparison. Notice the < symbol. When a company only says its sales exceed a certain amount, the most we can calculate is an upper limit for the multiple.

Where are the models built?

Stanford’s 2025 list associates 59 notable models with the US and 35 with China. Each square here represents one model. If you’re choosing one for a job, the count won’t settle it. Compare what you can access, how well it does the job and what it costs.

Map the concentration

Select a country to see how many models on this list are affiliated with it. The circle helps you locate the country. A single model may have ties to several countries, so adding the circles can count it more than once.
