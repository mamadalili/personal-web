Before You Delegate to AI Agents

A customer asks for a refund. An AI agent can find the order and draft a reply. Who gets to move the money?

Authority comes before speed

A customer writes, “Cancel my order and give me a refund.” A tool could draft a reply. Connect it to the ordering system and let it take steps there, and you have what this article calls an agent. Looking up an order, cancelling it and moving money are three different acts. Which of those should the agent be able to carry out?

Management belongs in the design

The programmer can’t settle that question alone. Support knows the returns policy, and finance knows who may issue refunds. BCG’s analysis argues for working across technology, finance and HR. NIST offers voluntary guidance on managing AI risks. These sources can inform a design. Neither has measured how this particular refund process works.

In the exercise below, the support team follows a single refund request on paper. Two colleagues reading the same sheet should be able to tell which action has happened and which still needs approval.

Make the authority agreement inspectable

Permitted inputs and outputs

An order number and payment status are relevant here. Does the agent need the customer’s other purchases? Specify the records it can read and where it puts its draft. Give the support specialist time to check the wording before the customer reads it as a promise.

Action boundaries and approval

Write down “view payment,” “recommend refund” and “issue refund.” The agent might handle the first two while a finance colleague authorizes the third. Put the amount, order and effect of approval in front of that colleague. A green status light gives them little chance of spotting a refund for the wrong amount.

Failure, stopping and handover

What if the order can’t be found, or a second message withdraws the cancellation? Name the person who takes over and where they’ll find the case. Show whether the money has actually moved or only a reply has been drafted. Otherwise the next colleague could issue the refund a second time.

The CEO and technology team need a shared problem

In the support example, the manager wants customers to get a clear answer without having to write repeatedly. That gives the technology team a problem it can work on. With “use a few agents” as the only goal, a demo could go perfectly while nobody knows how to handle a customer whose request appears twice.

Work through a duplicate request together. Support decides what the customer needs to hear. Technology shows how the agent finds an open case. Finance explains how to check for an earlier payment. If the departments disagree about what happened to a single order, settle the disagreement before granting permission to act.

How should we read BCG’s productivity figures?

BCG’s figures refer to the work and teams discussed in its analysis. For an estimate that fits your service, follow a request from the first message to an accepted reply. Include the time a finance colleague spends checking the amount or tracing a mistake.

Try it in your own operation first

BCG calls the technology team “customer zero.” The builders use the tool in their own work and encounter the gaps themselves. In the support pilot, put an order with a mistyped number beside an ordinary one. Can a colleague see why it stopped and pick up the case without calling the builder?

Note when the draft was ready, then when the customer received an answer they could use. The text might take moments while the reviewer spends much longer hunting down a payment receipt. Keeping those times separate shows which work got easier and which simply moved to another desk.

Stop a practice case halfway through. A colleague on the next shift should be able to tell what changed, what is still safe to do and which message the customer has already received.

Tie expansion to evidence

Before adding automatic cancellations or address changes, reopen a few trial cases with support. Which customers had to ask again? Did a reviewer correct a proposed amount? The answers tell you whether the next job concerns payment data, the wording of a reply or a responsibility nobody has taken on.

For each new task, go back to the people affected. Finance may need a clearer transaction history, or the support lead may have to set aside review time. Count that cost alongside the benefit. When granting a new permission, be able to say who will test it and how it can be withdrawn.

Test one boundary

An agent recommends a refund but has read-only permission. What comes next?

For a final check, pick a real request you have permission to review. Ask a colleague to take it through to completion using the sheet. If they still can’t tell who may issue the refund after reading the agent’s recommendation, fix that line and try it on another request.
