AI: More Work. Where’s the Return?

AI finished the first draft early. Why is everyone still working late? Follow one report from draft to sign-off to see where the time actually goes.

IN THIS EXPERIENCE

  • Before starting, decide whether you’re timing the draft or the finished report.
  • Count the time other people spend checking and fixing the work.
  • When the team saves time, track where it actually goes.

The draft is ready. Is the team finished?

The sales report is due this afternoon. With AI, a draft is on your desk before lunch. You still have to check returns against the sales file, and a colleague needs time to read the final version. What got faster? You saw the draft arrive early. To measure the team’s time, follow the work to sign-off. To measure profit, you also need the costs. The three options below let you look at each question separately.

Choose the scale

Pick the task, team or company. Each question calls for a different measurement.

Is there positive evidence too?

Yes. A QJE study using data from 5,172 support agents reports a 15% average increase in issues resolved per hour. That result concerns that occupation and setting, not profit growth at every firm. Read the paper.

More time is not more output

Where does freed-up time go?

Back to the sales report. This time the review is finished too, and you’ve handed it in early. Do you take on another report, check the source of a doubtful number more closely, or let the team finish on time? Each tab explores one choice and what you could track to see its effect.

More work

When the queue keeps growing

Accept another report straight away and you’ll have more drafts for the same colleague to check. To see the workload that creates, count the approved reports, the ones left in the queue and the times someone had to reply after hours.

Better quality

When saved time is reinvested

You might spend the time tracking down the source of a number. Later, see how many approved reports came back for a correction. That helps you judge what the extra checking bought you.

Sustainable capacity

When stopping belongs in the workflow

You can leave the slot open to let a colleague finish pending work, learn something or take a break. See whether the team delivers the same quality within the hours you agreed before increasing the queue.

How could you test this on your team?

Choose a recurring report whose reviewers know what good looks like. Record the usual process, then try AI on a comparable task. These four questions will help you set up a trial that tells you what to change next time.

When does the report count as finished?

Choose the finish line in advance. For example, log the time when the report is approved with its figures checked. Use that point for both methods and note errors found later. Differences in experience or task difficulty matter when you compare the runs.

What did it cost to get there?

You know what the subscription costs. Add training, setup, waiting, checking and rework. If an hour opened up, where did it go? A colleague catching a mistake and a colleague delivering another paid job create different kinds of value.

Work boundaries and a stop rule

Agree on work-in-progress limits, response expectations and review time. Specify in advance which errors or additional strain warrant limiting the trial. Do not change the criteria midway to make the result look better.

A decision at the scale of the evidence

If one task improves, report that result. To extend the claim to a team or company, examine that level’s costs and consequences too. Designing the measurement connects strategy, execution and AI workflows.

What can the trial tell you?

Daily tasks can change before a shift shows up in hours or earnings. In the March 2026 revision of the Danish study, researchers describe tasks being rearranged but detect no average effect on recorded hours or earnings. To read an effect across a group, check which outcome was measured and whose work is included.

Draft counts increased; review cost and business outcomes are unmeasured. Which conclusion is supported?

For the next report, record when it was accepted, what needed fixing, who fixed it and how the spare time was used. Next time someone asks whether the tool helped, you can answer for a specific piece of work.
