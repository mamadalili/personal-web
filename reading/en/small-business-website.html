What Should a Small Business Website Do?

Your washing machine breaks and you find a local repair service online. What would you want to know before calling? That question can help you decide what belongs on your own website.

Give the website a job first

Your washing machine is leaking. You find a repair service on your phone and open its website with two questions in mind. Does the technician work on this model? Will they come to your neighbourhood? A good-looking homepage leaves you stuck if you can’t find those answers. A shop has a different job. There, you might first want to know whether something is in stock and how returns work.

Red Website Design gives eight reasons to have a website, from showing your work to measuring results. Take credibility. For our repair service, it might mean showing a similar repair and introducing the person who will come to the customer’s home. That gives the owner something specific to put on the page.

If the technician has an appointment in your area, the page tells you so and someone answers the phone, the site has done part of its job. To judge what it was worth to build, follow that route in your own business.

Eight reasons, eight useful questions

Open “Credibility” and look at the page a new customer actually sees. Can they tell who will do the repair, or does the page just ask them to trust the company? Then pick a question that fits your business and try it on your own site.

Move from claim to evidence

Open one item, then look for the answer to that question on your own page.

Follow one person’s route

You’ve found your neighbourhood and your model and you’re ready to call. Where’s the number? Can you tap it on a phone? A customer may have made up their mind and still be stuck trying to reach you. The tabs show what someone needs before calling a service, buying from a shop or hiring a specialist.

Local service

Show coverage and proof early

Make the problem, service area, response method, examples and contact route easy to find. Test: can a phone visitor tell whether the service is available to them without an extra call?

Online shop

Reduce uncertainty before checkout

Specifications, trustworthy availability, delivery, returns and support shape the decision. Test: follow an order from product page to confirmation and note where you must guess.

Expert service

Make the work and your role legible

Explain the problem, your role and the delivered output in one project. Test: can a potential client tell whether a conversation about a similar problem is worthwhile?

Try the same exercise for a shop. Start on the product page and look for what a buyer needs before ordering. Then follow the order to see whether the team can deliver under the terms shown on the page.

Build trust with proof, not decoration

Back to the repair service. A photo of a repair, with the model and the fault underneath it, helps a customer see whether the technician has done comparable work. Get permission before sharing a photo or a customer’s words. If one repair took a day, describe that job without promising the same timing for every machine.

What if there are no case studies or reviews yet?

Give the customer something they can check today. Before they book, tell them which models you repair and what happens after they call. Once you have a real job to show, ask the owner’s permission and describe the fault and the work done.

Suppose the service stops covering a neighbourhood but the website still lists it. The customer finds out only after making the call. Ask the person who books repairs to flag changes in coverage, and agree on who updates the page when they do.

Do not confuse visits with outcomes

The repair page is getting more visits. Look at the calls from the same period. How many people asked about machines and neighbourhoods the technician covers? Could the team answer them? If most calls came from outside the service area, the visit count tells only part of the story.

Visits rose, but qualified enquiries did not. What is the more careful first reading?

Before changing the page, count the calls from neighbourhoods the service covers. After putting coverage beside the number, count calls using the same definition. A handful of calls or a change of season leaves the effect of the edit uncertain.

Where to start this week

This week, hand the repair page to someone who has never used the service. Give them a neighbourhood and a machine model. Ask whether they would use the page to arrange a repair. Watch without guiding them. If they go to the contact page looking for the service area, write that down.

Now you have a specific change to try. Put the service area next to the phone number and see whether the next visitor can answer the question on their own. To look at a purchase or enquiry on your own site, see how I work or tell me about the page.
