Several interests. Where do you start?

You can write, and you’re interested in web design. On Saturday you start a marketing course. Midweek you work on a shop’s web design. At the weekend you try a new AI tool. You’ve enjoyed all three. Now someone asks, “What could you help me do with those skills?” That can be harder to answer than picking the next course.

Hussain Ibarra starts with a similar question. When several things interest you, how much time do you give to learning one well? His suggestion is to develop real depth in one or a few areas and let other interests support that work. To try it, start with something you already do well and see where the job calls for another skill.

You don’t have to choose a job title for the next decade today. Picture a shop where a buyer wants to know whether a product fits in their kitchen. Keep that question in mind. It will help us see when the job needs a writer, who must confirm a dimension and how to tell whether the explanation helped.

Breadth needs something substantial underneath it

If writing is your strength, you might spot the problem with “suitable for small spaces.” How small? Which part of the kitchen should the buyer measure? That ability to judge the work and explain your reasoning is what we mean by depth here. A web designer might use the same kind of judgement to spot a dimensions table that’s unreadable on a phone.

A supporting skill helps you do that job better. To write the guide, you might look at the words people search for when they want a product’s size. Reading those questions doesn’t require mastery of technical SEO. If the site turns out to be missing from search results altogether, that might be a job for a specialist.

One domain only

The web pages work well, but there’s still a *sales* problem

The shop’s web design gets the menu and order button working. But the shopper still has a question: “Will this fit in my kitchen?” Without a way to find out, they may leave without ordering. To understand the sales problem, ask what’s making the decision difficult.

Several unfinished starts

Many tools, no usable result

For the same shop, we start a logo, video, analytics and a chatbot at once. Without a clear problem and boundary, none reaches actual use. The missing piece is a smaller, finished scope rather than another tool.

Depth with connections

Related abilities answer one question

We select a recorded customer question. Product understanding, clear writing and a usable help section work together. Then we check whether a user can find the answer without assistance. The combination is judged through that task.

Let a real problem choose the next skill

The buyer asks, “Will this fit in my space?” You could learn several AI tools, generate a pile of drafts and still have no answer. In this teaching example, first find out which dimensions to compare, who can verify the product measurements and where on the page the buyer expects to find them.

The next step depends on where you start. If you know the product, you may need practice explaining its dimensions to a first-time buyer. If you’re a web designer, find out from support which size question keeps coming up. If you write copy but lack the specification sheet, your next step is to ask the product owner for it.

For a first version, take one product and put a short guide beside its verified dimensions. Someone can now open the page and try measuring their own space. Watching that attempt can tell you whether to ask more about the product, change the wording or move the table.

Which bottleneck should your next skill remove?

In the tool, start with something whose quality you can already judge. A writer might be able to explain why “takes up little space” isn’t enough for a buyer. Next choose where your work is stuck. If none of the three starting points fits exactly, use the worksheet to describe your own.

Build a route from a skill you already have

Choose your working foundation and the bottleneck in front of you. Inspect the combination, add a real problem and download your worksheet.

Keep your starting skill and change only the obstacle. If you don’t know what buyers are asking, a conversation might help. If you know the question but keep abandoning the job, try finishing the guide for one product. If the guide is ready but nobody has read it, give it to someone to try.

AI helps more when you know what to inspect

Suppose you have the product width from its specification sheet and ask AI to tidy up the table. The draft says, “So it fits in a space this wide.” What if installation needs extra clearance? If the sheet doesn’t say, leave that question open for the buyer. Product knowledge helps the writer spot when a neat rewrite has quietly turned into an unsupported promise.

The copy and measurements might be accurate while only half the table is visible on a phone. Rewriting the sentences won’t help this time. Open the page and check whether you can read the width and depth alongside the right labels. If fixing it isn’t your job, you can now tell a colleague exactly where you need help.

Does combining skills guarantee protection from AI?

No. The source is confident about the future, but that does not establish job or income guarantees. Nor do we have a universal timetable for reaching a skill percentile. This page proposes making and inspecting work. Major career decisions also depend on your finances, market, experience and circumstances.

Make your work recognisable without inventing a persona

If you put the page in a portfolio, describe what you actually did. For example, “I found the buyer’s question, the product owner confirmed the measurement and I used AI to organise the table.” If nobody has used the guide to buy yet, you can’t tell whether it increased orders. The short account already tells someone where they could use your help.

You don’t need to publish a buyer’s name or a private team conversation to explain the decision. Where you have permission, describe the problem without personal details. “The buyer needed to distinguish the product’s dimensions from installation clearance” tells someone what the work involved without inventing a story or a result.

Ibarra also suggests setting your own criteria for taking on work. Before accepting, write down why you want the job. Perhaps income matters most this time, or perhaps you want to work with someone who understands product measurements. If it takes up all your time and leaves no room for your reason for doing it, having the skills is a poor reason to say yes.

Write so the work is understood, then consider a business

When the guide is ready, record the buyer’s question, what you put on the page, what you changed after the first feedback and what you haven’t checked yet. If someone asks to see your work later, that note can be a portfolio example. A new colleague can also read it to understand why the table ended up where it did.

Ibarra takes the idea towards a one-person business. You could do the same work within a team. If you’d like to charge for it, talk to a shop owner. How do they currently handle sizing questions? What would they pay someone to deliver? A good guide shows what you can make. The owner’s answer helps you find out whether they want that service.

If your next question is what to offer a client, continue with One person, many roles. If you know what you want to learn but haven’t tried it yet, Learn more than one skill by doing can help you choose a first exercise.

Choose one concrete next attempt

Read your worksheet against a deliverable such as “one sizing guide for one product.” Can you name someone who can verify the dimensions, and a buyer who could try using the guide to measure their space? If you’re missing an answer, follow it up. Keep the web design work to that one product until a buyer has tried the guide.

Is the next step clear enough to try?

Mark an item only if you can point to an example or a concrete explanation.

Watch where the buyer pauses when they open the guide on a phone. If they can’t tell what to measure, the explanation may need work. If the answer is clear but they can’t find the table, web design is the next skill to practise. Try putting the table where the buyer looks when choosing a size. Now the work itself gives you a reason to learn the next skill.
