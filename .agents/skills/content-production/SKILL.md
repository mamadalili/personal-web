# Content Production

Turn available material into content the intended reader can understand and use. Keep the connection between the reader's question, the author's evidence, the draft, the edit and the eventual outcome. A one-off post needs a good post, not a newly built content platform.

## Establish the brief from available context

Identify the audience, the question they need answered, the purpose of this piece and a suitable next action. Use the user's request, approved copy, existing project guidance and accessible notes before asking for more information. Ask only for a consequential gap that cannot be resolved from those materials. Respect existing review boundaries and explicit title or wording choices.

Keep the brief proportionate: enough context to explain what the reader should understand or do, which material supports it and what remains unknown. Do not prescribe a fixed interview, posting frequency, word count, folder tree or set of AI roles for every request.

## Build from evidence and the author's voice

- Track each consequential claim to an actual note, source passage, verified specification or calculation. Preserve units, dates, scope and uncertainty. Keep source observations, the author's experience, editorial proposals and hypothetical teaching examples distinguishable.
- Inspect the actual source before saying it was reviewed. Check changeable product capabilities, prices and technical instructions against current primary documentation. Claude chat, Claude Code, ChatGPT Projects and local-folder environments have different access; do not assume a filename, remembered chat or prompt creates access, a connection or a saved schedule.
- An unsupported fact becomes a question or is omitted. Do not fill missing dimensions, testimonials, experiences, financial results or performance with plausible inventions. A link alone does not establish that it supports a claim.
- Prefer the author's approved examples and corrections over imitating a source writer's voice. Explain with concrete subjects, familiar words and a clear point. Replace broad praise with the actual task, evidence or decision. Do not make every sentence terse or every opening imperative.
- Write original explanations and worked examples. Attribute source inspiration and substantive claims; respect quotation and paraphrase limits. A source's long prompt library or entire article is not a ready-made translation to republish.

For a concrete demonstration of brief, draft and scoped correction, read [the worked example](references/worked-example.md). Read it when developing examples or turning feedback into reusable guidance, not for every small text edit.

## Draft, inspect and choose useful interaction

Develop the argument before choosing a visual. Make each section answer part of the reader's question, explain the necessary reasoning and lead to an appropriate next action. Keep the requested depth; controls, banners and bullet lists do not replace substantial explanation. When useful, include a complete example from raw material through a reviewable output.

For an interaction, name what the reader changes or selects and what they learn from the change. Use only data and relationships that exist. A categorical handoff can support a workflow without supporting a numerical chart, map or dated Gantt. Label educational datasets beside the result. Keep exact values, method, units and a readable alternative; no invented percentages or automatic success states.

If artwork is requested, inspect references that can actually be accessed and state any access limitation. Do not claim an unseen image informed the visual composition. Keep topic/title, native-language lettering, relevant imagery and reference requirements coordinated. An in-body image should explain a passage or decision. Do not add a physical metaphor or decoration merely because a visual skill is available.

For native bilingual content, settle the meaning in the author's reviewed language first, then write the other version naturally with the same claims, examples, controls and limits. Do not use browser translation or introduce stronger promises in the second language.

## Make edits reusable without overgeneralizing

When the user asks to retain an edit, record its reason and scope in the existing approved writing guide or project notes. A useful rule includes the earlier wording, the correction, why it matters, where it applies and an exception or boundary. A buying-guide edit does not automatically become a rule for a personal essay or all future introductions.

Check for conflicting or duplicate rules before adding one. Retire or narrow obsolete guidance when later evidence warrants it. Store only what future work needs; keep private notes out of public repositories. Do not create a permanent rule from a preference the user has not endorsed, or describe saved instructions as retraining model weights.

## Review outcomes proportionately

If performance data is available, choose measures relevant to the piece's purpose. Compare matching definitions, denominators and observation windows. Missing measurements remain missing. An account-relative outlier identifies something to investigate; it does not establish causation, sales or ROI.

Turn observations into a specific next question or bounded experiment. Do not infer a universal winning format or create background routines from the existence of a content plan. Use the actual requested tool and current access; no provider or plan selection is fixed by this skill.

Deliver the requested reviewable content and identify material unresolved facts. Copying, exporting, saving, publishing and scheduling are distinct actions: follow the user's existing authorization and project workflow. The skill itself grants no external-action permission.
